# Manager meeting and feedback playbook

Use this reference for deeper meeting diagnosis or exact feedback language.

## High-bandwidth principles

- Put background information in a pre-read when possible.
- Spend synchronous time on decisions, disagreement, uncertainty, and feedback.
- Ask for the conclusion before the chronology.
- Distinguish a missing artifact from a debatable conclusion.
- Stop when the missing prerequisite is clear.
- End with a changed state: decision, narrowed question, owner, or corrected method.

## Bad case and better case

| Bad case | Why it fails | Better case |
|---|---|---|
| Give a broad task, then evaluate with hidden criteria | Creates surprise and rework | State the decision question and decisive evidence beforehand |
| Interrupt every detail immediately | Prevents structured reporting and hides whether the employee had an answer | Hear the top-line conclusion first, then challenge it |
| Repeatedly ask the same rhetorical question | Adds pressure without information | Name the gap once and request the corrective artifact |
| Recalculate and redesign everything live | Manager becomes the worker | Establish the missing standard and return ownership |
| Use sarcasm or global labels | Reduces truth and increases defensive agreement | Critique the observable artifact, impact, and next behavior |
| Soften an invalid conclusion to protect feelings | Corrupts standards | Say directly that it is not decision-ready and why |
| Mix review, brainstorming, coaching, and performance feedback | Nobody knows what success means | Name the meeting mode or transition explicitly |
| Continue after the work is clearly not ready | Produces low-nutrition discussion | Stop, define the correction, and reschedule the decision |
| Accept "yes, understood" | Creates false alignment | Ask for a short restatement of result, scope, and output |
| End with vague "research more" | Repeats the same loop | Set question, owner, artifact, timing, and acceptance condition |

## Direct language that preserves standards

Use:

- "This is not ready for a decision because..."
- "I do not need more breadth; I need an answer to..."
- "This claim is not yet a fact. What evidence would confirm it?"
- "Stop here. The missing prerequisite is..."
- "I am not going to rebuild this live. Return with..."
- "The result is invalid; the useful part we can retain is..."
- "I accept A. I do not accept B. C remains unknown."
- "The next review will evaluate these three things..."
- "If the scope cannot fit the time, propose the trade-off before the deadline."

Avoid:

- "A child could do this."
- "You did nothing."
- "You never understand."
- "Just think harder."
- repeated "yes or no" pressure when the underlying question needs evidence;
- reassuring language that obscures an actual failure.

## Protect the truth signal

Reward:

- early disclosure of a real unknown;
- correction of one's own error;
- a clear statement that evidence is insufficient;
- a justified challenge to the manager's assumption;
- a proposed scope trade-off.

Challenge:

- bluffing;
- agreement without understanding;
- hiding missing work;
- responsibility transfer to AI;
- unsupported certainty.

The goal is not emotional softness. It is a communication environment in which the manager receives accurate information soon enough to act.

## Stop conditions

Pause or end the review when:

- the required artifact does not exist;
- a decisive number cannot be reproduced;
- the employee has not understood the decision question;
- discussion has shifted to unsupported speculation;
- the manager is now doing most of the analysis;
- the same point is repeating without new evidence.

Use the remaining minutes to define the smallest correction, not to simulate another workday inside the meeting.

## Recent-meeting retrospective

Ask:

- What previous work entered the meeting?
- What trustworthy information left the meeting?
- What decision changed?
- What did the manager have to do that should have been employee pre-work?
- What manager behavior reduced clarity or truth?
- What will be different before and during the next meeting?
